2 Bishops Checkmate

The 2 Bishops Checkmate, different from the Queen and Rook Checkmate, requires more elegance to execute. Although the bishop is not as powerful as the rook or queen, two bishops together are a force to be reckoned with. This section will teach the most simple way to achieve a quick and easy checkmate with the bishop pair.


The 2 bishops checkmate takes the most effort to execute when the opponent's king is in the middle of the board, so we will consider that situation.


To accomplish this checkmate, we will wish to achieve the following steps:

  1. Place the two bishops in a line so that they form a "V-cage" to trap the opponent's king (see Figure 1.0). Move the king onto this line as well

  2. Use the king and the bishop to "drive" the opponent's king backwards towards the edge of the board. By this point, your king should be opposing the opponent's king and preventing them from escaping

  3. Use the two bishops to slowly take away the king's squares one at a time, and use your king to prevent the opponent's king from escaping the cage

  4. When the king is trapped in the two squares in the corner, use your two bishops to perform a "rolling" checkmate


To understand the strong collaboration between the two bishops to perform a checkmate, let us practice these steps in a real position:


Figure 1.0: White to Move


We have a very typical position for the 2 bishops checkmate. First, we should line up the bishops on the same line right beside each other to form a "V-cage", and we can do so in this position by playing 1.Bd3. Black has no good move to make with the king, so 1...Ke5 would be a natural move. White should follow up by placing the king on the same line as the bishops, so we should proceed with 2.Kc3, and a crucial position arises after 2...Kd5:


Figure 1.1: Position after 2...Kd5


This position is crucial to the 2 bishops checkmate, and is also step 2 of the checkmating process. We realize that our king cannot move forward anymore due to the strong position of the Black king, so we need to "drive" the king backwards. To do this, White should play 3.Bd4!, forcing the Black king back to the 6th rank with 3...Kc6 4.Kc4 Kd6 5.Be4, and we get a similar position once again except with the Black king being further back.

We will continue driving the Black king back with our bishops and our king, and a sample line would be 5...Ke6 6.Kc5! Kd7 7.Be5 Ke7 8.Bd5 Kd7 9.Bd6! Ke8 10.Be6 Kd8, resulting in the following position:


Figure 1.2: Position after 10...Kd8


Notice that as we slowly push the king backwards, the cage gets more and more limited although our bishops are staying on the same files. In this position, Black's king can only move back and forth between d8-e8. Now, we must follow through with the 3rd step, and slowly drive the Black king towards the corner of choice. Since our king is currently on the left side of the bishops, it would be easier to force the king to a8, although forcing it to h8 would also be possible after Kd5-Ke5-Kf6.

The fastest way to force the king to the corner, however, would to play 11.Kc6 Ke8 12.Bc5! (a waiting move) Kd8 13.Bf7! (taking away the e8 square) Kc8 14.Be7! (taking away the d8 square) Kb8 15.Kb6! (preventing the king from leaving the cage through a7) Ka8 16.Be6! (taking away the c6 square):


Figure 1.2: Position after 16.Be6!


By "rolling" the bishops, White is able to take away Black's squares one by one and force the king into the corner with only the a8-b8 squares to move between. This position is lucky since we don't need to make any waiting moves, but we wish to obtain this position as Black to move. If not, we can simply make a waiting move with one of our bishops such that the same c8-d8 squares are covered.

It is easier in this situation however, because after 16...Kb8 17.Bd6+ Ka8 18.Bd5#, White traps the Black king on a8 and checkmates!

That will be all on the 2 Bishops Checkmate. In the following section, we will consider more complex bishop endgames.
